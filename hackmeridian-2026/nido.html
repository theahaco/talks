Nido gives each of your users a C-address.

  • If you're new to Stellar, one thing first: Stellar has two kinds of accounts.
  • A G-address is an account with keys: one, or up to twenty with weights and thresholds. If you've used Ethereum, think of an externally owned account with multisig built in.
  • It can limit how many signatures an action needs, but not "this app may spend two XLM a day." The usual wallet is an extension and a seed phrase.
  • A C-address is a contract. Its code decides who can sign, which apps can act for it, which contracts they can call, how much they can spend, and until when. On Ethereum you'd call that a smart contract wallet.
  • Nido gives each of your users a C-address, behind one passkey.

  • Here's what that changes, starting with getting in.
  • The old way, a new user installs an extension, creates a password, writes down twelve words, switches network, and funds the account. Every step is a place people drop off.
  • With Nido, it's one passkey: Face ID or a fingerprint. Nothing to write down, and on testnet the account arrives with five test XLM.

Never locked out

Recovery

Off by default. Turn it on and choose:

Protect againstA lost passkey, or a stolen one
Who helpsTrusted friends, your recovery kit, or both
How manyFriends who must approve
TimingStandard: wait 7 days, then 14 days to finish
  • And if the user loses that passkey? Recovery is off until they turn it on.
  • They choose: a lost or a stolen passkey; trusted friends, a recovery kit, or both; how many friends must approve. Then a wait: seven days, then fourteen to finish.

The old way vs with Nido

Limits

The old way: no limit

The wallet signs whatever the user confirms. The only protection is reading every popup.

With Nido: a scoped key with a cap

The user approves a key for your app, and sets how much it can spend.

Spending capAn amount per day, week or month
ExpiryThe key stops working after it
Enforced byThe account, on chain

A call over the cap is refused before anything is sent.

  • And limits. A classic wallet signs whatever the user confirms, so the only protection is reading every popup.
  • With Nido, the scoped key for your app carries a spending cap, an amount per day, week or month, and an expiry.
  • The account enforces both, on chain. If your app, or anyone with its key, tries to spend past the cap, the call is refused before anything is sent.
  • That's the screenshot: the game at its 2 XLM a day cap. The guess stopped before the device was asked, and it cost nothing.
